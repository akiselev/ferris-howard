# FH Quantum Research Program: Verified Discovery

**Status:** Draft 0.1 · The original-research arm. Companions: quantum corpus, Atlas, agent layer, validation docs.
**Positioning:** AI-driven *heuristic* discovery in quantum information is now industrial (LLM-evolutionary code search at IBM/OmniQEC/Max Planck scale). We do not compete on candidate generation throughput. Our lane is **verified algebraic discovery**: results that ship with kernel-checked certificates, found by agents whose exploration is *directed by the Atlas* rather than by mutation — targeting the exact/structural questions the evolutionary crowd cannot close because "it works in simulation" is not their bottleneck's answer. Three durable edges: (1) the certificate pipeline (Rust search → Lean proof, inherited from the agent layer's anti-cheat protocol); (2) Atlas transport/frontier as a conjecture *generator* with a self-limiting noise property; (3) an accident of this project's history — the stabilizer formalism is symplectic geometry over 𝔽_p, so our finite-fields corpus and the quantum corpus are secretly about the same objects, and we own formalized statements on both sides of that dictionary.

## The pipeline (one loop, five stages)

Atlas (`frontier`, `transport`, `similar`) proposes a directed conjecture or search target → Rust-side engines (enumeration, SAT, SDP, numerics — the unfair-advantage layer) hunt candidates or counterexamples → surviving candidates get exact/rational reconstruction → Lean certifies (`decide`, constructed witness, or proof) under the full anti-cheat protocol (frozen statements, axiom audit, kernel recheck) → the result and its certificate enter the corpus, enriching the Atlas for the next loop. Failure at any stage is data: refuted transports mark dictionary boundaries; reconstruction failures flag numerically-fragile candidates the heuristic literature may be over-trusting.

## Target 1 — Certified small-code records (entry point; low risk)

The best-known-code tables for small [[n, k, d]] parameters carry entries where best-known ≠ best-possible, and the field just flooded itself with unverified candidates: hundreds of evolutionary/LLM-discovered codes whose distances are estimated, not proven. Two contributions, both squarely in our pipeline: *certify the flood* — run machine-checked distance certificates (the Lean-QEC SAT-reduction genre) over the published AI-found candidates, producing the verified subset and, plausibly, catching wrong distance claims, which is a publishable service to the field either way; and *close table gaps* — SAT/enumeration search targeted at specific open [[n, k, d]] cells, where any new code ships as a theorem, not a candidate. Risk: crowded on generation, empty on certification — we occupy the empty half. This target also stress-tests the whole pipeline on ground that cannot embarrass us: a certified code is a certified code.

## Target 2 — Stabilizer-rank lower bounds for magic states (active frontier; precedent exists)

The stabilizer rank χ(|T⟩^⊗n) governs the cost of classically simulating quantum computation; lower bounds are hard-won and small (linear-ish in n against an expected exponential truth). The genre "computational search + formal verification + LLM assistance" is already live here — current work in exactly this area acknowledges using Claude for Lean formalization of the supporting identities — meaning the method is validated and the frontier is incremental: better bounds for small n, new orbit constraints, qudit cases. Our additions: exhaustive verified search over stabilizer decompositions at small n (a `decide`-class computation once the F22 finite-matrix pattern lands), and Atlas `similar` probing rank lower-bound techniques against tensor-rank and matrix-rigidity clusters on the math side — a skeleton family where cross-pollination has historical precedent and formalized statements make it navigable.

## Target 3 — The quantum entropy cone at four parties (falsification-arm native; either outcome publishes)

Which linear inequalities constrain von Neumann entropies of multipartite states? Beyond strong subadditivity, constrained inequalities exist, and whether any *unconstrained* new inequality holds for four parties is open. This is the falsification arm's home game: candidate inequalities are generated combinatorially (mirroring the known classical/Shannon-cone machinery — a literal dictionary row, since the classical entropy cone's non-Shannon inequalities provide transport templates), and each candidate meets a counterexample search over parameterized state families (Rust numerics; stabilizer states first, since their entropies are computable exactly over our 𝔽_p machinery). A survivor with a proof is a new entropy inequality; a killed transport of a classical non-Shannon inequality locates precisely where quantum entropy diverges from classical — the "analogy boundary" output, publishable as such. Deliverable either way; that's the target's virtue.

## Target 4 — Certified Bell bounds (verified-NPA as novel infrastructure)

The quantum value of I3322 — the next Bell inequality after CHSH — is not exactly known; the best bounds come from the NPA semidefinite hierarchy, computed in floating point. Contribution ladder: (a) *verified NPA* — extract rational certificates from SDP solutions and check them in Lean, giving the first kernel-checked Tsirelson-type bounds beyond CHSH (infrastructure novelty even at known precision); (b) push levels/precision on I3322 and selected nonlocal games with certificates; (c) the structural prize, finite-dimensional ansatz search directed by Atlas `similar` against the operator-algebra cluster. Post-MIP*=RE, everyone knows the infinite/finite-dimensional boundary is where the deep questions live; certified bounds are how you walk near it without fooling yourself. Risk: SDP-to-rational reconstruction is fiddly at high precision — a known-hard engineering problem, but engineering, and ours.

## Target 5 — The moonshot: SICs, MUBs, and the number-theory dictionary (Atlas-native)

Zauner's conjecture (SIC-POVMs exist in every dimension) has an astonishing empirical structure: known exact SIC fiducials generate specific abelian extensions of real quadratic fields — a live, literal dictionary between quantum measurement theory and algebraic number theory (Stark units, ray class fields, Hilbert's 12th problem), i.e., between the quantum corpus and the Z-arithmetic corpus this project was born in. Program: formalize the SIC statement cluster and the number-theoretic side at statement level; `atlas dictionary` assembles the known rows; `transport` generates directed predictions (which field invariants should govern the next dimension's fiducial); exact-computation agents attempt fiducial construction/verification in dimensions where numerics exist but exact solutions don't — each success is a bona fide research result with an algebraic certificate. The MUB-in-dimension-6 problem rides the same infrastructure as the falsification-arm sibling (exclusion searches with verified case coverage). Risk: highest — this is where the field's own best people work — but it is the target the Atlas was *shaped* for, and partial outputs (formalized dictionary, transport-generated conjectures, verified exclusions) are contributions short of the summit.

## Target 0, running underneath — the 𝔽_p-symplectic frontier (the home-advantage probe)

Continuous background task rather than a headline: `atlas frontier` and `dictionary` between the finite-fields/function-field clusters and the stabilizer cluster. The known rows are rich (Weyl–Heisenberg over 𝔽_p, discrete Wigner functions, Clifford = affine symplectic, Galois qudits over 𝔽_{p^m}); the directed question our corpus is unusually equipped to ask is what the *unmatched* entries are — most enticingly, what the Frobenius endomorphism (the star of the math side, with a genuine action on 𝔽_{p^m} qudit systems) corresponds to on the quantum side, where it is barely studied. Any nontrivial transported theorem here is original mathematical physics discovered by the exact mechanism this project exists to test — which makes this the honest first experiment to run the day the Atlas passes its validation suite: low stakes, owned corpus, and if it produces nothing, that too is a calibration datum published with the tooling.

## Program-level honesty

Ordering is by risk: T1 → T2 → T3/T4 → T5, with T0 continuous. "Publishable" means: a certificate-carrying artifact plus prose, submitted through the norms of the relevant community with AI involvement disclosed (the Erdős-wiki etiquette, ported). The evolutionary-discovery crowd's velocity is a feature for us, not a threat — every unverified candidate they publish is raw material for the certification pipeline. And the standing epistemic rule from the agent layer binds everything: nothing is "discovered" until the kernel says so, and the kernel doesn't read press releases.
