# FH Physics Research, Round 2: The Ambitious Track

**Status:** Draft 0.1 · Extends `fh-quantum-research.md`. Round 1 was risk-ordered and stayed inside quantum information's well-lit rooms; this round leaves the building. Same non-negotiables — every claim certificate-carrying, every analogy transport-tested, partial results defined in advance — but the targets are chosen for *conceptual* upside: each one, if it works even partially, changes what the pipeline is understood to be. Graded A1→A6 by nearness of the first rigorous foothold, not by importance.

## A1 — Resource-theory dictionary mining (the four-way functor)

Physics has quietly built four-plus parallel theories with the same skeleton: entanglement theory, quantum thermodynamics, magic/stabilizer theory, and asymmetry — each with free states, free operations, monotones, conversion theorems, catalysis, second-law-like statements. The field *knows* these rhyme (the abstract "resource theory" framework exists) but has no systematic instrument for mining the rhymes: which theorems proved in one theory have unproven, true analogues in another? This is `atlas dictionary` and `atlas home` pointed at four corpora simultaneously — bound-minimization lifts each conversion theorem to its true home in resource-theory generality (a free generalization across all four), and unmatched entries are directed conjectures ("catalytic majorization has this magic-theory analogue; nobody has stated it"). Foothold: all four theories are finite-dimensional and statement-formalizable today; majorization is in Mathlib's neighborhood. Partial success that already publishes: the formalized four-way dictionary with its epistemic tags, plus one transported-and-proven theorem. This is the *most Atlas-native target in either round* — the machinery doing exactly what it was designed for, on a field that has pre-announced the analogies but never enumerated them.

## A2 — Axiom cartography of quantum mechanics (`atlas home`, pointed at physics itself)

Quantum foundations asks "which axioms does QM need"; we can ask the dual, mechanically: *which axiom does each quantum phenomenon need?* Formalize a lattice of generalized-probabilistic-theory (GPT) axioms (tomographic locality, purification, no-restriction, ...) and known foil theories — most importantly Spekkens's toy model, which reproduces teleportation, dense coding, and no-cloning-like behavior from epistemically restricted *classical* mechanics, and which stands in exact known correspondence with qubit-stabilizer QM (a proven dictionary row, and in odd prime dimensions the correspondence with our 𝔽_p machinery is exact). Then run bound-minimization on every theorem in the quantum corpus: teleportation's true home is far below full QM; contextuality's is not; the *map* of which phenomena live where is the deliverable — quantum foundations as a computed atlas rather than a debate. Foothold: GPTs are convex geometry over finite dimensions; Spekkens is combinatorial. Risk: axiom formalization choices are contestable — mitigated by shipping the lattice as data others can amend. Out-of-box payoff: this generalizes the pipeline itself — `atlas home` was built to find a theorem's algebraic home; here it finds a *phenomenon's physical* home, which is a new kind of output.

## A3 — Holographic codes: bulk/boundary as a literal, machine-checked dictionary

AdS/CFT is physics's deepest dictionary and rigorously out of reach — except in one corner: holographic stabilizer codes (HaPPY-type tensor networks), where the bulk-boundary correspondence, complementary recovery, and a Ryu–Takayanagi formula are finite-dimensional *theorems*. Program: formalize the holographic-code cluster (rides Round 1's stabilizer machinery and the F22 finite-matrix pattern); certify the known correspondence for the pentagon code — producing the first kernel-checked instance of a holographic dictionary, a headline in itself; then turn the search engines loose on the design space: holographic codes optimizing properties the evolutionary-QEC crowd doesn't target (exactness of RT, transversality, bulk locality), with every found code shipping as a theorem about a tiny holographic universe. Atlas angle: the bulk/boundary dictionary becomes *rows in our dictionary format*, and `transport` of bulk statements to boundary statements is executable, not metaphorical. Risk: the gap between toy holography and actual AdS/CFT is a chasm and must be stated in every abstract; the honest claim is "verified structural holography in the stabilizer regime," which no one currently has.

## A4 — Duality mining (industrializing the numerical coincidence)

Most known dualities began life as someone noticing two partition functions agree suspiciously well. That discovery mode — compute many, compare all — is precisely what machines do better: enumerate small statistical-mechanics and spin-model families (couplings over small lattices, qudit Clifford-adjacent models where our exact-arithmetic engines apply), fingerprint each by exact partition-function/spectral data, and hunt collisions across *structurally distinct* families. Collisions graduate through the pipeline: numerical coincidence → exact coincidence over a decidable ring → candidate duality map → certified duality on the finite family, with Kramers–Wannier as the mandatory calibration rediscovery (the benchmark discipline, ported: if the miner can't find the self-duality of the 2D Ising model when handed it, it can't find anything). The Atlas contributes the *directedness*: skeleton similarity between model families prioritizes which pairs to compare, replacing brute quadratic search. Honest odds: most collisions will be known dualities or trivial re-parameterizations; the value calculus is that ONE genuinely new finite duality pays for the program, and negative structure ("no nontrivial duality exists within family X at size ≤ n," certified) is itself a new kind of physics statement — dualities currently have no no-go literature at all.

## A5 — The universal positivity engine (one skeleton, four fields)

This conversation keeps meeting the same proof shape: Weil's RH-for-curves (intersection positivity), Connes's RH criterion (⟨Wf, f⟩ ≥ 0), the NPA hierarchy for Bell bounds (Round 1 T4), and the conformal/quantum-mechanical *bootstrap* — all instances of "positivity of a quadratic form pins the spectrum/theory." Round 1 builds verified-SDP certification for NPA; A5 is the recognition that the same engine, generalized, serves the bootstrap: rational certificate extraction for bootstrap exclusion bounds, starting with the quantum-mechanics bootstrap (anharmonic-oscillator spectra from positivity — finite moment matrices, honest reach) and scaling ambition toward certified conformal-bootstrap islands (the 3D Ising exclusion plots, currently floating-point artifacts of enormous consequence, as kernel-checked theorems). The Atlas's proof-shape index makes this target self-aware: the positivity skeleton is *indexed as such*, and the engine's applicability to a new field is a query, not an insight. Out-of-box framing that is also just true: this would make one piece of verified infrastructure load-bearing across number theory, quantum nonlocality, and CFT — the strongest possible demonstration that proof-shapes, not fields, are the right unit of tooling.

## A6 — The spectral realization program (full circle, eyes open)

The moonshot that closes the conversation's loop: treat Hilbert–Pólya as an *inverse spectral search problem* with a certification discipline. Not "prove RH" — three tracks strictly short of that, each with defined value: (i) *certified spectral mimicry*: systematic search over structured finite Hamiltonian families (quantum graphs, spin chains, Berry–Keating-type truncations) for spectra matching zeta zeros to height T, with exact statements "family F realizes the first N zeros to precision ε" — mapping which structures can and cannot carry the zeros; (ii) *certified no-gos*: theorems of the form "no Hamiltonian in class C (local, bounded coupling, dimension d) realizes the zero statistics beyond bound B" — no-gos are provable where existence isn't, they genuinely constrain the operator hunt, and essentially none exist in the literature; (iii) *transport auditing*: the physics-side RH literature (primon gas, the 2026 dynamical-phase-transition realization, xp regularizations) re-derived as verified dictionary rows, separating what these constructions *prove* from what they suggest — a service the field needs and, given the area's history of enthusiasm outrunning rigor, will not build for itself. Risk: maximal, and the mitigation is that every track's partial output is a publishable artifact whose value doesn't depend on RH yielding. If the Atlas's whole premise is right — that the missing operator is a missing *dictionary entry* whose shape is already determined by the rows around it — then the most rational way to hunt it is to formalize the rows and let directed search feel out the hole. And if the premise is wrong, the certified no-gos will say so, which is more than anyone can currently say.

## Round-2 honesty ledger

A1 and A2 are fundable-tomorrow tractable and are recommended to run interleaved with Round 1's T2/T3. A3's first milestone (certified pentagon code) is a bounded project with an unbounded headline. A4 and A5 are infrastructure bets whose worst case is excellent tooling. A6 is a moonshot governed by the no-go discipline. Across all six, the pattern from Round 1 holds and sharpens: the pipeline's comparative advantage is never raw discovery throughput — it is that *relationships, homes, and certificates are first-class objects*, and Round 2 simply bets that physics, being made of dictionaries, rewards that more than mathematics does.
