# FH Statistical Mechanics: Four Tiers

**Status:** Draft 0.1 · Distinct from the solid-state track by focus: stochastic thermodynamics, phase-transition rigor, and the nonequilibrium frontier — the parts of stat mech that are theorems about finite Markov chains and convergent expansions rather than lattice quantum matter.

## Tier 1 — Corpus and finite stochastic ground truth
Finite Markov chains with detailed balance; Gibbs measures and partition functions on finite graphs; free-energy identities; the exactly-solved anchors not already claimed by solid state (finite-chain master equations, birth–death processes, the two-state toy of every stochastic-thermodynamics paper) certified end-to-end. Infrastructure headline: **certified mixing-time bounds** for explicit small chains (spectral-gap enclosures via the certified-eigenvalue machinery) — the stat-mech analogue of certified band structures, consumed by every tier above.

## Tier 2 — Fluctuation theorems, kernel-checked
Jarzynski's equality and the Crooks fluctuation theorem are, for finite Markov chains with explicit protocols, *identities provable nearly verbatim* — among the most formalization-ready famous results in modern physics, and apparently untouched by any prover. Program: formalize both for finite chains, plus the integral/detailed fluctuation-theorem family, then the modern layer: **thermodynamic uncertainty relations** certified for explicit small systems (precision-dissipation trade-offs as checked inequalities). Deliverable: the stochastic-thermodynamics core as a certified library — the field's exam-question theorems become imports, and every experimental fluctuation-theorem test gains a machine-checked theory side.

## Tier 3 — Phase transitions with proofs
The rigorous phase-transition toolkit, formalized where it's most classical: the **Peierls argument** for the 2D Ising model — existence of a phase transition as a kernel-checked theorem (a genuinely landmark formalization target: bounded, famous, combinatorial-analytic); Lee–Yang circle-theorem statements with certified zero computations for finite volumes; and Pirogov–Sinai theory at statement-plus-first-instances level, sharing the cluster-expansion machinery (and its machine-optimized convergence radii) with the QCD track's C6 — one expansion engine, two fields. Exit: "the 2D Ising model has a phase transition" existing as a theorem object, which — remarkably — appears not to exist in any prover.

## Tier 4 — The nonequilibrium frontier
Where the field's open questions are already inequality-shaped: certified speed limits and generalized TURs (the bound-conjecture adjudication genre from strange metals, transplanted — several circulating "universal" nonequilibrium bounds have contested scopes, and the referee treatment of *what each claims* is a contribution before any proof); large-deviation rate functions certified for explicit models; KPZ universality at statement level as the corpus's frontier marker; and the handoff that makes this track load-bearing for the whole program — **certified coarse-graining**: Markov-chain lumping with error bounds and Mori–Zwanzig memory truncations as `Effective` morphisms, making stat mech the Effective Calculus's stochastic column and the natural testbed for the compressibility phase diagram's classical side. Ledger: T1–T2 are the fastest route to a publishable certified library in the entire subfield menu; T3's Peierls target is the season-scale landmark; T4 is steady-state research feeding the meta program.
