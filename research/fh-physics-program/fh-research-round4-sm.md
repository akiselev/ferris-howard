# FH Research, Round 4: The Standard Model Track — Certified Tractability

**Status:** Draft 0.1 · Extends Rounds 1–3.
**The honest license.** Nobody proves new theorems about the continuum Standard Model — the continuum SM doesn't rigorously exist (that's B5's decade and beyond). But the phrase "within specific bounds" unlocks an enormous and *underexploited* rigorous territory: everything about the SM that is lattice, finite, discrete, or bounded-regime is theorem-shaped, and the field that needs those theorems most — quantum simulation of gauge theories, QCD above all — currently runs on error bounds that are unverified, loose by orders of magnitude, and directly determine hardware resource estimates. A constant-factor improvement in a *proven* bound is worth real qubits. That is the Round 4 wager: certified simplification as a contribution to physics, where the certificate is the product. Precedent for certifying analytic inequalities at scale: Flyspeck — Hales' verified nonlinear-inequality machinery for the Kepler proof is the technological ancestor of everything below, and Mathlib-side interval arithmetic is the enabling substrate.

## C1 — Certified, optimized product formulas for gauge Hamiltonians

The Trotter-error theory (commutator-scaling bounds, Childs–Su et al.) turns simulation cost into a *provable function of the Hamiltonian's commutator structure* — and lattice gauge Hamiltonians (Kogut–Susskind) have highly special structure that generic bounds waste. Program: (a) formalize the product-formula error theory (finite-dimensional operator inequalities — squarely Mathlib-reachable); (b) turn bound-*optimization* into search — orderings, groupings, and higher-order schemes chosen to minimize the certified bound for the specific commutator algebra of SU(2)/SU(3) lattice models, with the optimizer free to be heuristic because the *output* bound is kernel-checked; (c) publish certified resource estimates for benchmark QCD-adjacent simulations, replacing the literature's "up to constants" with theorems. This is the purest instance of the Round-4 thesis: no new physics claimed, and yet the deliverable — provably-correct, machine-optimized simulation schedules — changes what hardware can reach. First milestone: certified second-order Trotter bound for the Schwinger model beating the generic bound's constant, end to end in Lean.

## C2 — Certified digitization: truncating infinite gauge links with proofs

Simulating QCD requires replacing each link's infinite-dimensional SU(3) Hilbert space with something finite — electric-basis cutoffs, discrete subgroups, q-deformations — and the "provably accurate" genre (rigorous truncation-error bounds for gauge and bosonic systems) exists but is young, mostly asymptotic, and untouched by formalization. Program: certified *explicit* cutoff-to-error functions (not O(·) statements — computable ε(Λ) with checked constants) for the standard truncation schemes; certified equivalence theorems between formulations (Kogut–Susskind ↔ loop-string-hadron ↔ discrete-subgroup digitizations, with exact gauge-invariant-sector dimension counts — the Gauss-law reduction is an error-*zero* proven simplification and the cheapest big win in the entire round); and search over digitization schemes scored by their certified error at fixed qudit budget. SU(2) first, SU(3) as the goal line. This is where "proven simplifications that make QCD simulation more tractable" is most literal: the physical-sector reductions and truncation certificates are the simplifications, and every one ships as a theorem.

## C3 — The flagship demo: an end-to-end certified quantum simulation

The Schwinger model (1+1d QED) is the community's drosophila — simulated on every platform, benchmarked endlessly, and never once with a *total* rigorous error budget. C3 composes C1 + C2 + tensor-network truncation certificates (1d entanglement bounds are rigorous territory) into one theorem: "this concrete circuit / this concrete classical contraction, with these resources, computes this Schwinger-model observable to within ε, kernel-checked, all error sources included." The first end-to-end certified simulation of an interacting gauge theory would be a genre-defining artifact — the simulation-physics analogue of a verified compiler — and everything it needs is finite-dimensional. Ladder: certified classical (MPS) budget first, certified quantum-circuit budget second, 1+1d SU(2) third. This target is also the program's best external handshake: it produces exactly the certificate a hardware group wants stapled to their next flagship experiment.

## C4 — Certified sign-problem cures

The sign problem is *the* wall between classical Monte Carlo and finite-density QCD. Deciding whether a Hamiltonian can be "cured" (made stoquastic by local basis change) is provably hard in general — which is precisely why the tractable version is search-plus-certificate: hunt over structured basis changes (Clifford, local orthogonal, symmetry-adapted) for specific model families, and when a cure is found, *certify stoquasticity* — a finite matrix-sign condition, kernel-checkable — turning "this model family is classically simulable after all" into a theorem. Complement with certified no-gos on restricted transformation classes ("no Clifford cure exists for family X," a finite check), mapping the sign-problem boundary the same way B6 maps decidability. Every certified cure is a proven simplification with immediate simulation payoff; every certified no-go justifies quantum hardware for that family. Both outputs publish, and the complexity-theory literature has already done us the favor of proving the general problem hard — so nobody expects miracles, only territory.

## C5 — Anomaly cartography: the Standard Model's arithmetic skeleton

The one place the actual SM — not a lattice proxy — is finite, discrete, and theorem-ready: anomaly cancellation. The hypercharge assignments of the SM satisfy a system of cubic Diophantine equations over the integers, and the space of anomaly-free chiral fermion contents and U(1) extensions is an *arithmetic* object — our founding machinery, pointed at particle physics. Program: certified exhaustive classification of anomaly-free charge assignments within explicit bounds (Diophantine search + kernel-checked exhaustion — exactly the certified-enumeration muscle from Rounds 1–3); certified treatment of the SM gauge group's global-structure ambiguity (the SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)/Γ quotients, Γ ≤ ℤ₆ — finite, discrete, and connected to which line operators the SM actually has); and Atlas `frontier` between this cluster and Z-arithmetic, because cubic Diophantine systems are number theory and nobody has systematically asked what transports. Any complete certified census here is simultaneously a particle-physics reference result and — in the round's most pleasing symmetry — the Standard Model meeting the ℤ-corpus this entire project started in. If a "new finding about the SM" is reachable this decade by our means, it lives in this discrete layer.

## C6 — Bounded-regime QCD theorems: cluster expansions and light cones, certified

The strong-coupling regime of lattice gauge theory admits *convergent* cluster expansions — genuinely rigorous statements (mass gap, exponential clustering, confinement-like area law at strong coupling) whose proofs are combinatorial-analytic and whose convergence radii are explicit but never sharpened, because sharpening by hand is misery and nobody's career rewards it. Machine-assisted, kernel-checked cluster expansions with *optimized* certified radii extend the region of parameter space where lattice QCD statements are theorems — literal new proven results about (lattice) QCD within specific bounds, which is this round's title implemented exactly. Same treatment for Lieb–Robinson velocities: certified, model-specialized LR constants tighten every downstream simulation guarantee and rigorous-physics argument simultaneously. Not glamorous; permanently citable. And it quietly extends B5's beachhead: every certified convergent expansion is constructive-QFT muscle memory for the φ⁴₂ program.

## Round-4 honesty ledger

Nothing here claims the continuum, the mass gap, or new particles. The claims are: certified error budgets where the field has estimates (C1–C3), certified simulability boundaries where it has folklore (C4), certified censuses where it has examples (C5), and certified expansions where it has misery (C6). Ordering: C1 and C2's first milestones are startable now and feed C3; C5 is independent, arithmetic, and the round's best fit to existing machinery; C4 and C6 are steady-state programs. The round's meta-observation, for the record: quantum simulation is the first *consumer* of this project's certificates that measures value in dollars-per-qubit rather than citations — which makes it the track where "proven, within bounds" stops being an epistemic stance and becomes a competitive advantage.
