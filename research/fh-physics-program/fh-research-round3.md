# FH Research, Round 3: The Far Ambition Track

**Status:** Draft 0.1 · Extends Rounds 1–2. Round 1 was entry-level verified discovery; Round 2 left the building; Round 3 is the horizon set. These are multi-year programs where the pipeline stops being a tool applied to research and becomes a claim about *how research works*. The honesty discipline survives intact — each target names its first bounded milestone, its partial-success ladder, and its precedent — because at this scale, discipline is the only thing separating a program from a manifesto. Ordered B1→B6 by how soon a real demo can exist, not by size of the dream.

## B1 — Abstraction synthesis: the Grothendieck move, automated

Everything so far treats a failed transport as an endpoint: record the dictionary gap, move on. The rising-sea methodology treats it as the *beginning*: rebuild the foundations until the gap becomes trivial — invent the concept that makes both sides instances. B1 automates that move. Formally: given two theories with a partial dictionary and a transported theorem that fails, search the space of trait-hierarchy extensions for the minimal new abstraction A such that (a) both theories are instances of A, (b) the failing theorem is provable at A's level, (c) A is nontrivial (its instance set exceeds the two inputs). This is anti-unification lifted from statements to *theories* — LGG over proof-and-structure pairs — with the Lean kernel as the acceptance oracle for every synthesized candidate.

The bounded demo is exquisite and this project has been rehearsing it since its first week: hand the system ℤ's unique-factorization proof and 𝔽_p[x]'s, with `EuclideanDomain` deleted from the environment, and require it to **re-invent EuclideanDomain** — synthesize the size-function-plus-division interface, verify both instances, prove the theorem once at the abstraction. If that demo lands, the same engine pointed at genuinely open gaps (the Frobenius hole, resource-theory meta-structure, the A2 axiom lattice) is running the most celebrated maneuver in modern mathematics as a search process. Partial-success ladder: rediscovery of known abstractions from Mathlib with the abstraction deleted (a whole benchmark suite hiding in Mathlib's own refactoring history — every historical "generalize this lemma" PR is a test case); then synthesis of unnamed-but-small abstractions humans would recognize as good; then the open gaps. Precedent honesty: concept invention has a graveyard (AM, HR, decades of it) — the differentiators are the kernel oracle and the benchmark-by-deletion methodology, which no prior system had.

## B2 — The classification program for small quantum theories

The 20th century classified finite simple groups; the analogous object for 2d quantum theories exists and is rigorous: rational CFTs correspond to modular tensor categories, MTC classification is honest finite mathematics (done by hand to low rank), and every extension of the classification is simultaneously a theorem in category theory and a census of possible physical theories. Program: formalize the MTC axioms (Mathlib's category-theory library is unusually deep — this is a real foothold), certify the known low-rank classification (a Lean-checked "there are exactly these theories" is already a publication), then push the frontier with the search engines: classification is constraint-solving over finite data (fusion rules, S/T matrices, pentagon/hexagon equations) — SAT/Gröbner territory where certified exhaustion is our exact specialty and where hand-classification stalls on case explosion. Headline framing that is also literally accurate: *a machine-checked census of small quantum universes*. Partial ladder: axioms formalized → rank ≤ 4 certified → any new rank classified. Round-2 A3 (holographic codes) and this target eventually meet, since MTCs are where topological order and anyon physics live — the census doubles as a catalogue of possible topological quantum computers.

## B3 — Is quantum mechanics an island? (reconstruction, completed by search)

A2 maps which axioms each phenomenon needs; B3 inverts the search: over the space of GPT axiom-sets, characterize *everything* that satisfies natural desiderata — and determine whether quantum theory is uniquely carved out, one of a family, or a point on a continuum. The reconstruction literature (Hardy; Chiribella–D'Ariano–Perinotti; Masanes–Müller) proves individual routes to QM by hand; nobody has run the space *exhaustively*, because exhaustive axiom-space search with per-theory verification was never mechanizable before. Finite foothold: fix small dimension and finite outcome structures, where GPTs are polytopes and cones — enumerable, checkable, and the falsification arm's home geometry. Deliverables ascend: certified reproductions of known reconstruction theorems (transport-auditing that literature, which contains real gaps); the map of near-quantum foils (theories satisfying all-but-one axiom — each is a publishable object, and some may be *new physics candidates as foils*); and the island verdict at small dimension, whatever it is. This is the "why quantum?" question converted from philosophy into a computed atlas with error bars, and even its partial outputs reframe the foundations debate around artifacts instead of intuitions.

## B4 — The formal referee: certified adjudication of contested no-go theorems

Quantum foundations contains theorems whose *content* is disputed — most famously Frauchiger–Renner, where a decade of literature argues about what the paradox actually proves, which assumption the contradiction indicts, and whether the reasoning is even valid. FR is a finite protocol with finite agents and finite reasoning steps: fully formalizable. B4 formalizes the contested no-gos (FR, PBR's ψ-ontology theorem, Kochen–Specker variants, the newer Wigner's-friend inequalities) at full logical resolution — every assumption a named hypothesis, every inference kernel-checked — and publishes the *dependency structure*: exactly which assumption-sets yield the contradiction, which don't, and where published rebuttals attack premises versus inferences. The pipeline's `home` operation then does something delicious: compute each paradox's minimal assumption set, mechanically settling "what does FR actually require" — the precise question the literature has spent ten years litigating in prose. This is a new *genre*: the formal referee, adjudicating not open problems but open *disputes*. It is also the most socially combustible target in three rounds — the mitigation is that we publish dependency graphs, not verdicts, and let named-hypothesis structure do the arguing. First milestone is bounded: FR formalized, both main published readings expressed as different hypothesis sets, the difference exhibited as a diff.

## B5 — The first machine-checked interacting quantum field theory

The continuum, at last, honestly. Constructive QFT rigorously built interacting theories in 2d — φ⁴₂ exists, Glimm–Jaffe wrote it down fifty years ago — and no interacting QFT has ever been machine-checked. B5 is the FLT-project analogue for physics: a blueprint-driven, community-scale, agent-accelerated formalization of φ⁴₂'s existence and Wightman/OS axioms, through the actual analytic spine (Gaussian measures, Nelson estimates, OS reconstruction — measure theory and functional analysis where Mathlib is strongest). Why this belongs to *us*: the blueprint methodology (Tao/Buzzard-style dependency graphs of lemmas) is precisely an Atlas object, agent swarms are precisely what makes a 10⁵-line analytic formalization tractable this decade, and the Clay-problem road (Yang–Mills existence and mass gap) *runs through here* — 4d Yang–Mills is not reachable, but the community that eventually reaches it will stand on the first machine-checked interacting QFT, and someone is going to build that artifact within the decade. Partial ladder: free field formalized → OS axioms and reconstruction → φ⁴₂ measure constructed → Wightman verification. Every rung is a first. Precedent: FLT's formalization was called impossible at this scale until it was funded and simply began.

## B6 — The computability phase diagram of physics

Cubitt–Pérez-García–Wolf proved the spectral gap undecidable; MIP* = RE welded quantum correlations to the halting problem. These landmarks imply something no one has drawn: physics has a *computability phase diagram* — regions of model space where physical questions are decidable (with extractable certified algorithms), regions where they are not, and a boundary whose shape is unknown and physically meaningful. B6 maps it: formalize the undecidability constructions' statement layer; below the line, ship *certified decision procedures* for physical properties in restricted classes (gapped-ness for commuting-projector models, phase equivalence for small stabilizer families — verified algorithms being a deliverable our pipeline uniquely ships); above it, tighten embeddings to push undecidability down to smaller local dimensions and simpler interactions; at the boundary, the new science. Out-of-box payoff: this treats decidability as a *physical property of theories*, on par with symmetry or phase — and the pipeline is the only instrument that can survey it, because surveying requires producing and verifying both algorithms and impossibility proofs at scale. First milestone: one certified decision procedure for one honest model class, which is simultaneously Round-1-grade work and this program's opening move.

## Coda: the loop out of the library

One thread deliberately left for last, because it is where all three rounds point: several targets (Round 1's Bell bounds, Round 2's duality mining, B3's foil theories) naturally emit *finite experimental protocols* — inequalities with certified quantum violations, noise-robustness margins as theorems, foil-discriminating measurements. A result format of "kernel-checked protocol + robustness certificate + lab-ready parameters" would let the pipeline's outputs leave the library and meet apparatus, which is the only exit criterion physics ultimately respects. That interface — theorems with lab handles — is not a Round 3 target; it is what Rounds 4+ are for, and the reason to keep the certificates honest now.

## Round-3 honesty ledger

B1's deletion-benchmark demo and B4's FR milestone are startable this year and are the recommended pair (one tests whether the machinery can invent, the other whether it can referee). B2 and B6 are multi-year with continuous publishable rungs. B3 is a five-year program whose early outputs already matter. B5 is decade-scale community infrastructure that should be *proposed* (blueprint + funding case) rather than merely attempted — its existence proof is that FLT's formalization was once this sentence. Across the round, the meta-claim being tested is the conversation's oldest one: that mathematics and physics are made of dictionaries, homes, and shapes — and that a system which makes those first-class can do more than assist the work. It can, sometimes, do the move.
