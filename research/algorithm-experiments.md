# Algorithms Over the Proof Stream: A Pre-Registered Battery for Agent-Driving Methods

**Status:** Draft 0.2 · Supersedes the graph-mining framing. Subject: novel algorithms applied to the *live artifacts* of the FH/Lean pipeline — goal states, elaborated terms, proof-term DAGs, tactic logs, certificate computation graphs — evaluated for one thing only: **do they change what an agent can do next?** Existing formalizations (Mathlib files, PhysLean) appear strictly as testbeds: elaborating them regenerates the exact artifact streams our campaigns produce, with the human proofs as ground truth for scoring. Every experiment: algorithm, substrate, falsifiable hypothesis, protocol with baselines, agent-relevant metric (goals closed, compute per goal, statement-match rate), and an honest account of failure. Nothing below is claimed to work; that is what the experiments are for.

---

## X1 · Missing-lemma synthesis by higher-order anti-unification over stuck-goal clusters

**Algorithm:** cluster the goals an agent fails on (by term-skeleton), compute least-general-generalizations (higher-order anti-unification) per cluster, and propose each LGG as a candidate missing lemma. **Testbed protocol (runnable today):** take an existing formalized file; delete one mid-tier lemma; re-elaborate — the downstream failures produce exactly the stuck-goal stream a real campaign would; run the synthesizer on that stream. **Metric:** statement-match rate — over M deleted lemmas (M ≥ 100, sampled across algebra/analysis/PhysLean, list frozen first), how often is the deleted statement recovered up to definitional equality among the top-3 LGGs? Baselines: most-frequent-subterm heuristic; retrieval of the nearest existing lemma. **Hypothesis, held loosely:** recovery meaningfully above baselines on single-deletion; we genuinely don't know whether LGG noise swamps signal when clusters mix goals with different causes — the multi-deletion condition (delete 3 lemmas at once, see if clustering separates them) is where failure is most likely and most informative. **If it works:** stuck agents gain a "synthesize what's missing" move — arguably the single most valuable capability an autonomous prover can have. **If it fails:** the failure mode analysis (do LGGs overgeneralize to triviality, or undergeneralize to the instance?) directly calibrates B1's harder ambitions.

## X2 · Equality saturation on stuck goals

**Algorithm:** e-graph saturation (egg-style equality saturation) over a goal's term plus its local equational context, extracting either a closure or a maximally-simplified restatement. **Substrate:** goal states, live. **Testbed:** the equational strata of existing files — strip terminal steps from closed equational proofs, present the resulting goals. **Metrics:** closure rate and proof-length ratio vs. `simp`/`rw`-search baselines at matched compute; plus the agent-relevant secondary: on goals *nobody* closes, does the saturated restatement measurably raise downstream closure by a standard agent (A/B)? **Prior-art note for the writeup:** e-graph/Lean integrations exist in early forms; the pre-registered contribution is the matched-compute comparison and the restatement-value measurement, not the idea. **Uncertainty:** associativity/commutativity blowup is the known killer; the experiment includes an e-class-size budget arm precisely because saturation may simply not scale on Mathlib-sized simp sets, and finding *where* it stops scaling is the publishable content either way.

## X3 · Proof-shape transfer: retrieve-and-replay

**Algorithm:** index proof terms by shape (the Atlas skeleton machinery, pointed at proofs); on a new goal, retrieve the nearest proof-shape and *replay its tactic skeleton* with holes re-elaborated in the new context. **Testbed:** held-out-file protocol — for every theorem in a held-out file, retrieval runs over the remainder; scoring against the human proof that exists. **Metrics:** closure rate vs. `exact?` and a standard automation baseline at matched compute; skeleton-source distance (does transfer work only within-topic, or across?). **Hypothesis:** above-baseline closure within-topic; cross-topic transfer is the low-confidence claim that would matter most (it is the transport thesis in miniature, at proof rather than statement level), and we specifically do not predict it succeeds. **Agent payoff if positive:** stuck-state handling gains "how do proofs of this shape usually go" as an executable move rather than a metaphor.

## X4 · Nogood learning: CDCL for proof search

**Algorithm:** conflict-driven learning ported from SAT — when a search branch fails, extract a *failure fingerprint* (goal skeleton × tactic prefix) as a nogood; prune future branches matching learned nogoods within the campaign. **Substrate:** tactic logs and failure traces, live — this experiment runs *on our own campaign data* by design. **Protocol:** fixed target set (a frozen list of unproven-by-baseline goals from elaborating an existing physics file), A/B agents with and without the nogood store, five seeds, matched compute. **Metric:** goals closed per unit compute; nogood store hit-rate; and the hazard metric, pre-registered because it is the likely failure — *wrongful pruning rate* (goals closable only through a pruned prefix), measured by replaying pruned branches offline. **Honest stance:** proof search is not SAT; clause learning's magic depends on resolution structure proofs may lack, and a null here with a clean wrongful-pruning analysis is a real result about why.

## X5 · Compression-driven lemma discovery

**Algorithm:** common-subproof extraction over a corpus of proof-term DAGs (grammar-induction/LZ-style compression on trees); every extracted frequent subterm is a candidate lemma/definition. **Testbed with built-in ground truth:** run over the proof terms of an existing file family *with its actual lemma structure hidden* (inline everything first); score extracted candidates against the lemmas the human authors actually factored out. **Metrics:** precision/recall against the human factoring; plus prove-out of *novel* candidates (extracted subproofs the humans didn't name — are they provable, general, and reusable, per agent adjudication with a frozen rubric?). **Why this one is load-bearing:** it is the §7 compression-selection thesis tested operationally on real data at the proof level — if MDL-style extraction can't even recover human factoring from inlined proofs, corpus-compression as an axiom-discovery principle needs revising, and better to learn that on a file than on a foundations program.

## X6 · Canonicalization for the proof cache

**Algorithm:** canonical forms for goals via term-DAG canonicalization (alpha-invariance, symmetry orbits, instance-normalization) as the cache key transform for S1's proof cache. **Substrate:** the goal stream, live. **Testbed:** elaborate an existing library, log every intermediate goal, and measure the *deduplication factor* — how many distinct raw goals collapse under each successive canonicalization layer — then the operational metric: cache hit-rate uplift in a re-elaboration and in a fresh agent campaign over related targets. **Hypothesis:** meaningful collapse from alpha/instance normalization (likely), meaningful *additional* collapse from symmetry orbits (genuinely unknown — mathematical goal streams may not repeat up to symmetry as often as intuition claims, and measuring that frequency is itself the finding). Cheap, immediately deployable if positive, and its null is a design fact S1 needs either way.

## X7 · Abstract interpretation over elaborated terms

**Algorithm:** abstract domains run over Lean terms — an interval domain over numeric subterms and a dimensions domain over physics declarations — as a static analysis producing warnings before any proof is attempted. **Testbed:** PhysLean and our own corpus files with *seeded faults* (unit mismatches, magnitude-absurd constants, inverted inequalities), per the §17 methodology. **Metric:** seeded-fault detection rate and false-positive rate at frozen thresholds, against the junk-value linter alone as baseline. **Agent payoff if positive:** the diagnostics payload gains a pre-proof plausibility layer — goals flagged dimensionally-inconsistent route to statement-suspicion (H2) *before* burning proof search, complementing falsification triage from the static side. **Uncertainty:** abstract domains famously drown users in false positives; the FP-rate arm is the experiment.

## X8 · Blame localization as a flow problem on certificate DAGs

**Algorithm:** treat the debugger's interval-trace blame assignment (`fh-debugger.md` §4) as a min-cut/flow problem on the certified computation DAG — enclosure-growth as edge capacity, corrupted inputs as sources — and compare algorithmic localization against the frame-scan baseline. **Substrate:** entirely our own artifacts — certificate computation graphs from extracted checkers. **Protocol:** seeded-corruption campaigns (single- and multi-site corruption of LDLᵀ-class certificates), localization accuracy and time-to-localize vs. linear frame scan. **Hypothesis:** flow-based localization wins decisively on *multi-site* corruption (where linear scan is ambiguous) and roughly ties on single-site; the multi-site claim is the uncertain, valuable one. **If it works:** the debugger's acceptance test gets an algorithmic core, and every future track's certificate format inherits a localization guarantee.

## Battery protocol

Frozen target lists, thresholds, and rubrics ledgered before first run; matched-compute comparisons everywhere (agent-driving claims are claims about *compute allocation*, so unmatched baselines are meaningless); every A/B on live campaigns runs ≥5 seeds; nulls reported with failure-mode analysis in the same table as positives; and the deployment rule that keeps this honest — an algorithm graduates from experiment to agent-tool only on a pre-registered win, and enters the toolchain *with its measured operating curve attached*, because an agent handed a tool without its false-positive rate has been handed a superstition. Expected outcome, stated plainly: several of these will null. The battery is designed so that each null is a citable finding about where a celebrated algorithm's assumptions and proof search part ways — which is, for a program that intends to industrialize agentic mathematics, exactly the map that needs drawing first.
