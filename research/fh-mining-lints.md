# FH Mining, Grading, and Lints: The Effective-Content Toolchain

**Status:** Draft 0.1 · Three connected designs: proof-mining transforms as FH tooling, a grading system that detects Sundman–Wang-class "absurd" programs before they waste anyone's decade, and the lint catalog — including the linearity lint that Rust's costume makes nearly free.

## 1. Mining transforms: what they are, and the FH program

**The objects.** A mining transform is a *proof interpretation*: a systematic, mechanical translation of proofs in one system into proofs in another that makes hidden computational content explicit. The classical toolkit: the **negative (double-negation) translation** (embeds classical logic into intuitionistic — a classical proof of a Π₂ statement becomes an intuitionistic proof of a classically-equivalent one); **Friedman's A-translation** (upgrades that to extract genuine witnesses from classical Π₂ proofs); **Gödel's Dialectica (functional) interpretation** (translates each statement into a "there is a functional beating every challenge" form whose realizer is an explicit program — the deepest of the family); and Kohlenbach's **monotone and bounded functional interpretations**, the modern workhorses, which extract *uniform bounds* rather than exact witnesses — crucial, because bounds survive classical reasoning that witnesses don't. The composite pipeline (negative translation ∘ functional interpretation ∘ majorization) is what "proof mining" means in practice.

**Why this is our research direction and not a curiosity.** Kohlenbach's school has run this pipeline *by hand* for twenty-five years, extracting explicit rates from nonconstructive proofs in approximation theory, ergodic theory (metastability rates of the kind Tao popularized), fixed-point theory, and — most recently and most relevantly — convex optimization, where mined convergence rates for algorithms are competitive research output. Three facts make it ours: (i) every mined artifact is *literally an Effective Calculus object* — a rate/modulus with a regime is an error functional with a pedigree, so mining is the calculus's acquisition arm for classical mathematics; (ii) the transforms are syntax-directed and mechanical in principle but executed by human experts in practice — the gap between "mechanical in principle" and "mechanized" is exactly an agent-plus-metaprogram gap, and nobody has built industrial mining tooling over a modern library; (iii) the **metatheorems** (below) are a static analysis, which is a compiler feature wearing a logic costume. **FH program:** `fh mine <theorem>` — apply the interpretation pipeline to a target proof, emit the extracted bound as a new theorem-carrying function (a §8-style promotion with the mining trace as pedigree), register its rate in the Effective library. Milestone ladder: MN1 — Dialectica/monotone interpretation as a Lean metaprogram on a toy fragment (quantifier-free + Π₂ arithmetic); MN2 — mine one real Mathlib convergence proof into an explicit modulus and cross-check against the literature's hand-mined rate (the answer-key discipline: hand-mined results are our oracle table); MN3 — mining as a standing service the Effective Calculus calls when the library lacks a rate it needs. Honest scope: full automation over arbitrary Mathlib proofs is a research program, not a milestone — but *semi*-automated mining with agent assistance on targeted proofs is plausibly the cheapest genuinely-novel contribution in the whole toolchain, because the competition is a small number of human experts doing it by hand.

## 2. Grading effective content: absurdity detection

The Sundman–Wang failure mode — a valid proof whose extracted program is computationally void — is detectable, and partially *predictable*, by combining one exact science with two heuristic instruments:

**(a) The static predictor: principle audits (exact-ish).** The deep result of applied proof theory is that Kohlenbach-style metatheorems *bound the growth class of extractable rates by the logical principles the proof consumes*: proofs within certain fragments guarantee primitive-recursive or even polynomial moduli; each use of stronger principles (full comprehension, unrestricted choice, nested bar recursion) admits correspondingly faster-growing extracted functionals. We already track exactly the needed data — the axiom audit and the bound-minimization index know which principles a proof uses. So the **principle audit** reads a proof's dependency signature and emits a *predicted complexity ceiling* for anything mined from it — a static warning, before extraction, that this existence theorem can at best yield a tower-class modulus. This is a real static analysis with theorems behind it, and shipping it as a compiler pass would be a first.

**(b) The structural heuristics (cheap, indicative).** Features over the statement and proof term that correlate with void content: quantifier-alternation depth of the statement (Π₂ mines well; Π₃-and-up needs metastable reformulation first — the lint can *suggest* the metastable version); induction-nesting depth and recursion pattern of the extracted term (nested induction → hyperexponential smell); presence of "compactness laundering" (WKL-style arguments that classically hide unbounded searches). None exact; all cheap; all rendered as diagnostics-payload hints with the usual applicability grading.

**(c) The empirical grader (the decisive one).** Every extracted program in our stack is an anytime evaluator — so *run it on an ε-ladder and fit the cost curve*. `fh grade` executes the artifact at ε = 10⁻¹...10⁻ᵏ, fits cost(ε), and stamps the **effective-content grade**: `POLY` (cost ~ poly(1/ε) or better), `EXP`, `TOWER`, `VOID` (cost curve unmeasurable at any useful ε — the Sundman–Wang stamp, assigned empirically without needing the asymptotics proven). The grade lands in the (error, regime, cost) triple as measured data, the Pareto surface gets honest coordinates, and `atlas envelope` learns to *rank candidate compressions by grade* — absurd solutions filtered before composition, which is the practical payoff: the envelope operation never again returns a technically-correct answer that takes 10⁸⁰⁰⁰⁰⁰⁰ terms. Calibration: Sundman–Wang itself, formalized at statement level with its known convergence rate, is the VOID-class reference standard — the founding parable promoted to a unit test.

## 3. The lint catalog

Grouped by what each refuses to let compile silently. Existing lints assumed (junk-value V10, Ruling-B parens, emittable-subset, units, `#[latex]` presence); new ones below, ★ = highest priority.

**★ L-LIN — the linearity lint.** Rust's ownership is an affine type system and FH wears Rust's surface — so quantum-state values can be declared `#[linear]` and the *frontend* enforces no-cloning discipline (no implicit copy, single consumption, explicit `.clone()` forbidden on linear types) even though the constraint erases in Lean. Measurement consumes its state; unitaries take and return ownership. This will not catch every semantic cloning (it's a lint, not linear logic), but it makes the no-cloning theorem a *compile-time habit* in quantum code, and it is nearly free: the borrow-checker mental model is already the surface's native physics. **★ L-RIGOR — rigor-tag monotonicity.** A `#[rigor(certified)]` declaration may not depend on a `#[rigor(statement)]` one without an explicit, ledgered `#[rigor_boundary]` acknowledgment — trust tiers propagate like `unsafe`, and silent laundering of statement-level assumptions into certified results becomes impossible. (This is Part V's trust graph, enforced locally at elaboration time.) **★ L-REGIME — regime-use.** Calling an `Effective` artifact's compressed model at inputs where its regime predicate isn't established (hypothesis in context, or decidably checked) is an error, not a hope — the where-clause gets teeth at every call site. **L-HERM — obligation synthesis for physical interfaces.** Declaring an operator `#[observable]` auto-generates the self-adjointness obligation; `#[state]` on a density-matrix generates trace-one and PSD obligations; `#[pmf]` generates normalization. Physical-role attributes stop being comments and start being proof debts. **L-SYM — declared-symmetry respect.** A definition tagged with a symmetry (`#[invariant(rotation)]`, `#[nondim]`) gets a metamorphic obligation: the invariance stated as a theorem (provable or todo-tracked), turning Part III's metamorphic testing into a static requirement for tagged declarations. **L-BASIS — basis-leakage.** Results about operators tagged basis-independent may not mention a concrete basis outside an explicitly-marked computation block — the classic silent sin of physics formalization, caught syntactically. **L-CONV — convention pinning.** Metric signature, spinor conventions, Fourier normalization: declared once per module via convention attributes, and any definition mixing modules with clashing conventions fails loudly — the Couette-normalization lesson generalized to every convention physics fights about. **L-COMP — the computability boundary.** Every public definition is stamped computable/noncomputable in its signature rendering, `noncomputable` leakage into extraction paths is an error at the boundary rather than a surprise at link time, and the choice heat-map (per-declaration classical-axiom provenance) ships in the diagnostics payload — the CH fine print, made visible everywhere it's about to matter.

## 4. Integration

Mining feeds the Effective library; grades feed the cost axis and the envelope ranking; the principle audit feeds both mining (what's extractable) and grading (what to expect); the lints feed the diagnostics engine as FH0xxx codes with the standard agent payload. First patients: MN2 mines a Mathlib convergence proof; `fh grade` stamps the couette tail-bound evaluator (expected: POLY, and if not, that's a finding); L-LIN lands with the first quantum-corpus elaboration; L-RIGOR and L-REGIME land with M0 because they are one attribute-walk each. The through-line, stated once: Curry–Howard's gaps were §1's subject, and this document is the engineering answer in triplicate — mine what classical proofs hide, grade what extraction yields, and lint what the surface should never have accepted.
